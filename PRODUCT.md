# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A Clinical Care Manager at Philadelphia's Community Behavioral Health program — the city's Medicaid mental health insurance provider. Manages 5 caseworkers who go out into Philadelphia neighborhoods connecting residents with mental health resources and support. Opens this dashboard on Monday mornings to orient for the week. The primary questions are geographic: where are referrals concentrating, which sites serve the most people in each neighborhood, how long are people waiting at specific offices, and what type of care does each area need. Secondary: which clients need immediate attention (no contact, Medicaid expiring, on the waitlist) and how is the team holding up. Not a data analyst; a field-oriented practitioner whose work is organized around people and place.

## Product Purpose

The Supervisor Snapshot is a Monday morning orientation tool anchored in geography. The supervisor's primary intelligence needs are place-based: where are referrals concentrating, which sites have the longest waits, what care is needed in specific neighborhoods. Layered on top: client risk flags (no contact, Medicaid expiring, waitlisted) and team capacity (caseload per caseworker, referral trends). Success means a supervisor can open it, understand what needs attention today and where in the city it's happening, and close it in under five minutes — with confidence, not anxiety.

## Brand Personality

Calm, caring, clear. The interface should feel like a trusted colleague giving you a thoughtful briefing, not a corporate metrics platform demanding your attention. Warm but precise. Human but structured.

## Anti-references

- Corporate BI tools (Tableau, Power BI) — dense, impersonal, data-first
- Clinical EMR/EHR systems — cold, sterile, transactional
- SaaS dashboard templates (any "Modern Admin Dashboard" UI kit) — generic, gradient-heavy, metric-tile spam
- Salesforce — bureaucratic, overwhelming, visually loud
- Charity website aesthetic — overly soft, stock-photo-warm, patronizing

## Design Principles

1. **Always show the human behind the number.** Counts are people. Every metric should make it natural to ask "who are they?" and drill into the list.
2. **Geography is the primary lens.** The supervisor's work is organized by neighborhood — where referrals come from, which sites serve each area, what care those neighborhoods need. Every aggregate stat should have a place-based dimension available. The Philadelphia map is the organizing metaphor for the whole dashboard, not a chart in a section.
3. **Monday morning brain.** This is opened at the start of the week, possibly before coffee. Minimize cognitive overhead. What needs action should be obvious without reading.
4. **Context over data, especially geographic context.** A number without comparison is noise. Trend, benchmark, or place must accompany every metric. "Average wait time: 18 days" is incomplete; "longest wait: North Kensington (31 days)" is actionable.
5. **The interface disappears into the work.** No decorative chrome. Design serves the supervisor's task, not the other way around.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Keyboard navigable. All charts have text alternatives. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning. Reduced motion preferences respected.
