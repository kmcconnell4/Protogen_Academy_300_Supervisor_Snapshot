# Product

## Register

product

## Users

A supervisor at a non-profit community mental health organization in Philadelphia. Manages 5 caseworkers who serve individuals receiving mental health benefits through Medicaid across multiple neighborhood sites. Opens this dashboard on Monday mornings to orient for the week — assessing client risk, team capacity, and community patterns before the day begins. Not a data analyst; a practitioner who cares deeply about the people and neighborhoods in their caseload.

## Product Purpose

The Supervisor Snapshot is a Monday morning orientation tool. It surfaces client risk (no contact, Medicaid expiring, waitlisted), team capacity (caseload per caseworker, referral trends), and community context (care needs by Philadelphia neighborhood, sites serving the most people). Success means a supervisor can open it, understand what needs attention today, and close it in under five minutes — with confidence, not anxiety.

## Brand Personality

Calm, caring, clear. The interface should feel like a trusted colleague giving you a thoughtful briefing, not a corporate metrics platform demanding your attention. Warm but precise. Human but structured.

## Anti-references

- Corporate BI tools (Tableau, Power BI) — dense, impersonal, data-first
- Clinical EMR/EHR systems — cold, sterile, transactional
- SaaS dashboard templates (any "Modern Admin Dashboard" UI kit) — generic, gradient-heavy, metric-tile spam
- Salesforce — bureaucratic, overwhelming, visually loud
- Charity website aesthetic — overly soft, stock-photo-warm, patronizing

## Design Principles

1. **Always show the human behind the number.** Counts are people. Every metric should make it natural to ask "who are they?" and drill into the list.
2. **Neighborhood and caseworker are first-class citizens.** The work is place-driven and person-driven. These two dimensions should be navigable, not buried.
3. **Monday morning brain.** This is opened at the start of the week, possibly before coffee. Minimize cognitive overhead. What needs action should be obvious without reading.
4. **Context over data.** A number without comparison is noise. Trend, benchmark, or explanation must accompany every metric.
5. **The interface disappears into the work.** No decorative chrome. Design serves the supervisor's task, not the other way around.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Keyboard navigable. All charts have text alternatives. Color is never the sole carrier of meaning. Reduced motion preferences respected.
